Assemble the handoff

Collect the journey record, design-record.yaml with its my_call entries and backlog, the release and discovery records, the isolation decision, the operations records and the resource inventory. Keep failed checks and open gaps beside the successful results. Link to sanitised records; never copy tokens, passwords, raw keys or unnecessary prompt content.

Accepted versus unresolved

Keep four states apart: proposed, implemented in the package, executed in your account and accepted by an owner. A diagram or a paper exercise never supplies a missing execution record.

Activity: review and transfer Design · 10 min

Make the handoff usable without you
  1. recordChoose a recommendation for the HR agent: ready for the stated audience, requires further qualification or retire this candidate. State the evidence and what would change it. Wider reuse reduces duplicate work but creates support obligations; a retained pilot costs money until someone owns the next decision.
  2. recordAsk a teammate to use only your handoff to find the approved version, one successful and one failed or pending check, the recovery target and who can authorise clean-up. Fix every missing pointer. Working alone, do the read-through without your terminal history.
  3. recordTransfer one decision to your organisation: change one requirement (a domain needs independent ownership, a team uses another model client, a customer needs a stronger tenant boundary), reopen the component decision record, compare the current choice with a supported alternative and define its acceptance checks: permitted and denied access, compatibility, failure and recovery, cost or performance.
  4. recordReconcile your points with Progress, evidence and points: enter points only against evidence; pending activities stay pending.

Guide prompt: "Given my changed requirement and current decision card, recommend what to keep and change. Identify the starting configuration, trade-off, implementation gap and evidence required to accept the change." Worksheets: journey record, evidence checklist.
